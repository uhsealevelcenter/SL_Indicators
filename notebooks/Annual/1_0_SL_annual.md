In this set of indicators, we explore how sea level is changing over time, using (mostly) yearly data. The analyses focus on both relative and absolute sea level and are organized by timescale, meaning we look at changes that happen slowly over many years as well as changes that happen within a year or from day to day.

```{margin} What do we mean by timescales?
Sea level does not change in just one way or one speed. Some changes happen very slowly, over many years or decades. Other changes happen with a year, and some happen from day to day.

In this book, we separate sea level changes by timescale:
- - **long timescales** show gradual changes over many years
- - **seasonal timescales** show patterns that repeat every year
- - **short timescales** show day-to-day ups and downs

 By looking at these timescales separately, we can better understand why sea level is sometimes higher or lower than usual, and which kinds of changes matter most for the extreme events in our region.
```



Each section is linked to a Jupyter notebook that shows how the analysis is done. These notebooks can be run step by step, allowing users to follow the methods and apply them to other locations.



The main questions we address are:
1.	**How fast is sea level changing at my location or in my region over the long term?** [(*Trend*)](../Annual/1_1_SL_Trend_annual.ipynb)
2.	**How does sea level move above or below that long-term pattern over time and across different places?** [(*Anomaly*)](../Annual/1_2_SL_anomaly_annual.ipynb)
3.	**When were the highest and lowest sea levels recorded, and how high or low were they?** [(*Rankings*)](../Annual/1_3_SL_Rankings_annual.ipynb)
4.	**How often do very high sea levels occur—every year, every few years, or only once in many decades?** [(*Annual Extremes*)](../Annual/1_4_1_SL_Extremes_annual.ipynb)
5.	**How do regular seasonal changes affect estimates of rare high-water events, such as a “20-year” high sea level?** [(*Nonstationary Extremes*)](../Annual/1_4_2_1_SL_Extremes_nonstationaryGEV.ipynb)
6.	**How do estimates of extreme sea levels change during different climate conditions, such as El Niño or La Niña?** [(*Return Level Variation by Climate Index*)](../Annual/1_4_2_2_SL_Extremes_nonstationaryGEV_ClimateIndex.ipynb)
7.	**How well do our estimated extreme sea levels match what has been observed in the past?** [(*Rankings Summary*)](../Annual/1_5_ranking_and_return_level_summary.ipynb)
8.	**How do changes at different timescales combine to create extreme sea level events, and how does this change over time?** [(*Contributions to Sea Level Extremes*)](../Annual/1_6_1_SL_contributions_to_extremes.ipynb)
9.	**Which timescales matter most for high and low sea levels on a daily basis at a given location?** [(*Contributions to Daily Sea Level*)](../Annual/1_6_3_contributions_to_daily_sea_level.ipynb)
